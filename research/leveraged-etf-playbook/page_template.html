Day-trading plan · 3x leveraged ETFs · tested on every session, Jan 2000 – Oct 2026

3x Morning Playbook

Each morning the bot runs four checks, then picks one of three plays or stays in cash. It buys at the opening bell with the whole account, exits by the close, and stops out at a 5% loss. It never holds overnight.

Stage by stage

The morning routine

The stages run in this order every trading day. Most mornings end in cash: the bot trades on about one day in seven, and only when the calendar or the tape has historically leaned one way.

  1. STAGE 0before 9:00

    Circuit breakers

    • If the account is down 10% or more since Monday's open, the bot is locked until next Monday. Stop here.
    • Set today's stop at −5%. If the week is already down, use the tighter level that would put the week at −10% (week down 8% → today's stop is −2.2%).
  2. STAGE 1before 9:00

    Calendar

    • Is today the monthly options-expiration Friday (third Friday)? → candidate ↓OPEX FADE
    • Is today one of the first 3 trading days of the month? → candidate ↑MONTH START
  3. STAGE 2before 9:00

    Market state at yesterday's close

    • Has SPY closed lower 3 or more days in a row? → candidate ↑DIP BOUNCE
    • Keep MONTH START only if SPY's 20-day volatility is between 15% and 40% (annualized standard deviation of the last 20 daily % changes). Calmer than that and the edge doesn't cover costs. Wilder than that and the stop sits inside normal noise.
  4. STAGE 39:30 open

    Opening check: don't chase

    • Drop DIP BOUNCE if SPY opens more than 0.3% above yesterday's close. The bounce already happened overnight.
    • Drop OPEX FADE if QQQ opens more than 0.3% below yesterday's close. The drop already happened overnight.
    • If a bull play and a bear play both survive, stay in cash.
  5. STAGE 49:30 → 4:00

    Execute and manage

    • Bull play: buy SPXL (3x S&P 500) with 100% of the account at the open. Bear play: buy SQQQ (3x short Nasdaq-100).
    • If the position falls to today's stop, sell everything. The bot is done for the day.
    • Otherwise sell everything at the close (3:59 pm ET, or a market-on-close order). Nothing is held overnight.

    What can go wrong

    01

    Years of going nowhere

    From mid-2015 to early 2020 the playbook barely grew while SPY kept climbing. Five-year stretches ending 2016–2020 returned 0.8% to 6.1% a year against SPY's 6.4% to 12.8%. The longest time below a prior high was 571 days.

    02

    Deeper drops than history shows

    The worst historical drop was −31%, and the worst month was −27.8% (Dec 2018). Across 5,000 reshuffled histories, the median worst drop was −38%, and 14% of them hit −50% or worse.

    03

    Stops are not guarantees

    A stop becomes a market order. In a flash crash, a trading halt or a market-wide circuit breaker it can fill far below −5%. A 7% S&P drop that halts the market is about −21% on a 3x fund. The test assumes 0.25% slippage; at 2% the edge shrinks to 10.5% a year.

    04

    All-in, 3x, every trade

    Putting the whole account in one 3x fund means one extreme day decides the year. An index drop of about 33% in a session would wipe out a 3x fund entirely. The bot depends on the stop and on the day staying orderly.

    05

    The edges can fade

    These are published, well-known patterns. The playbook earned 23% a year in 2000–2012 and 14% in 2013–2026, roughly even with SPY including dividends. If more traders crowd in, the edge can shrink further.

    06

    Account rules and taxes

    Margin accounts under $25,000 get 3 day trades per 5 sessions. The playbook needs 4 or 5 in about 2% of weeks, so use a cash account or keep $25k+. Every gain is short-term income, and quick re-buys after losses can trigger wash-sale rules.

    For whoever builds the bot

    Method and bot spec

    # runs once per trading day; all inputs are known by the 9:30 opening print
if week_pnl <= -10%:                      stay in cash until next Monday
stop = max(-5%, 0.90 / (1 + week_pnl) - 1)

bull = None
if trading_day_of_month in (1, 2, 3) and 15% <= spy_vol20 <= 40%:
    bull = "MONTH_START"
if spy_down_closes_in_a_row >= 3 and spy_open / spy_prev_close - 1 <= +0.3%:
    bull = "DIP_BOUNCE"
bear = (today is the 3rd Friday) and (qqq_open / qqq_prev_close - 1 >= -0.3%)

if bull and bear:   cash
elif bull:          buy SPXL with 100% of equity at the open
elif bear:          buy SQQQ with 100% of equity at the open
# intraday: sell all if position return <= stop, then done for the day
# 3:59 pm ET: sell all; never hold overnight

    Data

    Robinhood daily bars for SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA, Jan 3, 2000 to Oct 27, 2026: 6,744 sessions with no gaps, split-adjusted. Real SPXL/SPXS (since Nov 2008) and TQQQ/SQQQ (since Feb 2010) were used to check the model.

    3x model

    Before the funds existed, each 3x day is rebuilt from the index with the funds' daily-reset math. Against the real funds the model correlates 0.96 (SPXL) to 0.998 (TQQQ). It charges the measured gap of 0.02% a day on top.

    Costs

    0.05% per round trip (fund drag plus spread), plus 0.25% extra on every stop-out. The stop is checked against the day's low (bull) or high (bear). Without intraday bars, the exact fill time is unknown.

    How rules were chosen

    About 60 candidate patterns were screened. A rule survived only if it worked in 2000–2012 and in 2013–2026, in both SPY and QQQ. Thresholds sit in the middle of ranges that all worked. The Russell 2000 and Dow tests above were run once, with no changes.

    Benchmark

    SPY buy-and-hold from the same start, price only. Dividends would add roughly 1.8 points a year. Cash between trades earns nothing in the test, though a sweep account would add interest.
